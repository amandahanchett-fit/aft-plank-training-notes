Plank Training for the Army Fitness Test

The plank is the last strength event on the Army Fitness Test, and it rewards patience more than raw power. You hold a rigid body line for as long as you can, and the clock decides your points. A steady plan will move your time further than hard but random sessions.

Know the standard

Place your forearms on the ground with elbows under your shoulders, then lift your hips so your body forms one straight line from head to heels. Keep your neck neutral, your glutes tight and your ribs pulled down. The grader will end your attempt when your hips sag or pike, when you shift your feet or hands, or when your form breaks and does not return after a warning.

Why holds end early

Most people do not fail because of weak abs. They fail because the lower back takes over, the shoulders tire, or the breathing turns shallow and panicked. Breathe slowly through the nose and out through the mouth, and think about pushing the floor away with your forearms. A small posterior pelvic tilt keeps the trunk locked without strain.

A simple weekly plan

Train the plank three times a week on non consecutive days. On day one, do five holds of forty five seconds with a minute of rest. On day two, do side planks of thirty seconds per side and dead bugs for three sets of ten. On day three, do three holds that are each about seventy percent of your best time, then finish with one all out hold. Add five seconds to each hold every week. Every fourth week, take a lighter deload week and test yourself at the end.

Accessory work

Hollow body holds, bird dogs, and slow mountain climbers build the same trunk stiffness without the same shoulder fatigue. Add glute bridges, since strong glutes help you keep the hips level. Stretch hip flexors and chest after each session so your posture does not collapse under fatigue.

Test day habits

Warm up with two short holds of fifteen seconds. Eat a light meal two hours before, wear shoes with a firm grip, and pick a spot on the ground to look at so your neck stays quiet. Do not start with a record attempt in mind. Settle into the position, count your breaths, and break the hold into small blocks of ten breaths.

Track your score

Write your time after each test and compare it with your age band. When you want to see the points a time earns, you can look up the scoring tables.
